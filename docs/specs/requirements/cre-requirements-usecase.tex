\author{Suppakorn Krabtong}
\date{v1.15 --- 28 November 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\noindent\textbf{Scope.} CRE is a web application that assesses construction risk for projects on Thai islands. A user describes a project and answers four questions for each of 55 indicators in a versioned assessment; a dashboard summarises all own projects and versions can be compared; the Risk Engine computes likelihood ($\RL$), impact ($\RIC$, $\RIT$, $\RIE$) and the risk magnitude $\RM$, classifies each indicator as VL/L/M/H/VH, shows expert advice for high risks, and produces a PDF report. Administrators maintain users, advice and thresholds, and export pseudonymised data for research.

\noindent\textbf{Sources.} Risk Engine \& Indicator Reference v1 (11 Nov 2026); Architecture Decisions v2.8 (11 Nov 2026); product owner decisions (10, 14 and 23 Nov 2026, \S\ref{sec:dec}); UI wireframes and hi-fi design review (23 Nov 2026). Items marked \assume{} are assumptions not yet confirmed.

\noindent\textbf{Priority.} MoSCoW: \textbf{M} must, \textbf{S} should, \textbf{C} could.

\subsection*{UC-01 Register account}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\columncolor{grp}\raggedright\leavevmode\bfseries\color{creBlue}}p{2.6cm} Y@{}}
\toprule
Actor & Guest; secondary: E-mail Service, Google Sign-In \\
Precondition & Not logged in. \\
Main flow\newline (e-mail) & 1.~Guest opens the registration page and chooses sign-up with e-mail.\newline 2.~Guest enters e-mail, password, password confirmation and (optionally) organisation; no personal name is requested.\newline 3.~System shows the research data-retention notice and the terms of use; guest accepts both (NFR-03, FR-51).\newline 4.~System validates e-mail format and uniqueness and the password policy.\newline 5.~System generates a 6-digit OTP, stores it hashed with an expiry of 10 minutes, and sends it via the E-mail Service.\newline 6.~Guest enters the OTP.\newline 7.~System verifies the OTP, creates the account (role \texttt{user}, e-mail verified, hashed password) and logs the user in. \\
Alternative\newline (Google) & 1a. Guest chooses \emph{Continue with Google}: system redirects to Google Sign-In (OAuth 2.0 / OpenID Connect).\newline 1b. Google returns a verified e-mail and account id; guest accepts the notice and the terms of use (step 3); system asks for the optional organisation.\newline 1c. System creates the account (no password, no OTP) and logs the user in. If the e-mail already belongs to an account, the Google id is linked to it. \\
Exceptions & 3a. Guest does not accept the notice or the terms of use: account is not created.\newline 4a. E-mail already registered: show error; flow returns to 2.\newline 4b. Password fails policy (BR-22) or the confirmation does not match: show the rule; flow returns to 2.\newline 6a. Wrong OTP: show error; after 5 wrong attempts the OTP is invalidated.\newline 6b. OTP expired or not received: guest requests a new one (at most once per 60 s).\newline 1d. Google sign-in cancelled or fails: return to the registration page. \\
Postcondition & Verified account with role \texttt{user}; stored: e-mail, password hash and/or Google id, optional organisation. Nothing is stored before verification. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{minipage}

\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\subsection*{UC-02 Log in / reset password}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}>{\columncolor{grp}\raggedright\leavevmode\bfseries\color{creBlue}}p{2.6cm} Y@{}}
\toprule
Actor & Guest; secondary: E-mail Service, Google Sign-In \\
Precondition & Account exists and is active. \\
Main flow & 1.~Guest enters e-mail and password and may tick \emph{keep me logged in}.\newline 2.~System verifies the password hash.\newline 3.~System issues a JWT in an httpOnly, Secure, SameSite cookie (\texttt{path=/}); its lifetime follows BR-23.\newline 4.~System opens the dashboard (UC-20), which holds the project list (UC-05); an admin also sees the admin menu. \\
Alternative & 1a. Guest chooses \emph{Continue with Google}: system validates the Google ID token, finds the account by Google id (or verified e-mail) and continues at step 3. \\
Alternative\newline (forgot password) & 1c. Guest selects \emph{Forgot password} and enters the account e-mail.\newline 1d. System sends a 6-digit OTP via the E-mail Service (BR-16) and shows the same message whether or not the e-mail is registered.\newline 1e. Guest enters the OTP and a new password with its confirmation on one screen (BR-22).\newline 1f. System verifies the OTP, checks the password policy, stores the new hash, ends all existing sessions of the account\assume{} and returns to step 1. \\
Exceptions & 2a. Wrong e-mail or password: generic error (does not reveal which); flow returns to 1.\newline 2b. Too many failed attempts: request rate-limited (NFR-02).\newline 2c. Account deactivated or deleted: access denied.\newline 1b. No account for this Google identity: offer registration (UC-01).\newline 1g. Wrong or expired OTP: as BR-16; password unchanged.\newline 1h. E-mail has no password (Google-only account): the e-mail tells the user to continue with Google instead\assume{}. \\
Postcondition & User is authenticated. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

\subsection*{Risk Engine (from the Risk Engine \& Indicator Reference v1)}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.6cm,labelwidth=1.4cm,font=\bfseries\color{creBlue}]
\item[BR-01] Scoring a numeric input $v$ with thresholds $a<b<c<d<e$:
\[
s(v)=\begin{cases}
1 & v\le a\\
1+\frac{v-a}{b-a} & a<v\le b\\[2pt]
2+\frac{v-b}{c-b} & b<v\le c\\[2pt]
3+\frac{v-c}{d-c} & c<v\le d\\[2pt]
4+\frac{v-d}{e-d} & d<v\le e\\
5 & v>e
\end{cases}
\qquad \RL=s(X),\ \RIC=s(y_1),\ \RIT=s(y_2).
\]
$\RL$ uses the indicator's own $a..e$; $\RIC$ and $\RIT$ use one shared table each (seed: $\RIC$ 9.58, 23.92, 47.5, 61.42, 80; $\RIT$ 13.79, 23.33, 46.67, 60.83, 79.33). Inverse indicators (EC-MG-5, EC-CO-5, EC-RS-1, EV-EV-4) have $a>b>\dots>e$ with mirrored comparisons. Three-threshold indicators: only $a,b,c$ (EC-HS-2, EC-HS-5; $\RL=5$ above $c$) or only $c,d,e$ (EC-HS-1, EC-HS-3, SO-PL-1, EV-EV-2, EV-EV-3; $\RL=1$ below $c$, 3 at $c$). Scores stay in $[1,5]$.
\item[BR-02] $\RIE = \max_{o \in S} s(o)$ over the ticked options $S\subseteq\{A..I\}$; option scores A--C $=1$, D--E $=2$, F $=3$, G $=4$, H--I $=5$ (fixed, not editable). A (very little impact) is not combined with other options.
\item[BR-03] $\RImax = \max(\RIC,\RIT,\RIE)$ and $\RM = \sqrt{\RL \cdot \RImax}$, using scores rounded to $p_s$ decimals (default 2; environment variable, BR-18).
\item[BR-04] $\RM$ is rounded to $p_m$ decimals (default 3; environment variable, BR-18) and classified by the membership functions peaking at 1--5: VL $[1,1.5)$, L $[1.5,2.5)$, M $[2.5,3.5)$, H $[3.5,4.5)$, VH $[4.5,5]$. A value exactly on a border takes the higher level.
\item[BR-05] Advice is shown iff level $\in\{\lv{H},\lv{VH}\}$ (i.e.\ $\RM\ge3.5$).
\item[BR-06] Advice list:
$\RL$ list if $\RL \ge \RImax$; otherwise the list of the impact equal to $\RImax$. Comparisons use the rounded scores.
\item[BR-07] Tie between impacts for $\RImax$ (and $\RL < \RImax$): show every tied list, order $\RIC,\RIT,\RIE$.
\end{description}

\subsection*{Versions and data}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.6cm,labelwidth=1.4cm,font=\bfseries\color{creBlue}]
\item[BR-08] A project has versions $v_1,\dots,v_n$ ($n\ge0$); numbers are sequential per project and never reused. A new version is always blank.
\item[BR-09] An indicator is complete when $X$, $y_1$, $y_2$, $y_3$ are all given. A version may be submitted when at least one indicator is complete; not all 55 are required. On submission only complete indicators are scored ($\RL,\RIC,\RIT,\RIE,\RImax,\RM$, level); scores are shown only after submission. Status: \emph{draft} (never submitted) $\to$ \emph{submitted}; the latest version can be edited and resubmitted (BR-11).
\item[BR-10] A new version stores a snapshot of the project value and dates, every indicator's RL thresholds, the $\RIC$/$\RIT$ tables and $p_s$, $p_m$. All its calculations use the snapshot; threshold and project edits apply only to versions created afterwards.
\item[BR-11] Only the latest version of a project is editable. Once a newer version is created, earlier versions are read-only, even if the newer one is later deleted\assume.
\item[BR-12] Deleting a project or version is a soft delete (\texttt{deleted\_at}): it disappears from every user view and API response; no row or file is removed and there is no restore. Retained data is reachable only through the research export (UC-18).
\item[BR-13] Deleting an account (by the user or an admin) removes the e-mail, password hash and Google id and marks the account deleted. Its projects and versions are kept for research.
\item[BR-14] Research export is pseudonymised: no e-mail or Google id; each account is replaced by a stable random pseudonym not derived from its e-mail. Organisation is kept\assume.
\item[BR-27] Limits, set in the environment: an account may own at most \texttt{MAX\_PROJECTS\_PER\_USER} projects (default 3) and a project may have at most \texttt{MAX\_VERSIONS\_PER\_PROJECT} versions (default 10; no source gave a number, so 10 is a working choice). Soft-deleted projects and versions (BR-12) do not count, so deleting one frees a slot; version numbers are still never reused (BR-08). An over-limit create is rejected with HTTP 409 (\texttt{project\_limit\_reached} or \texttt{version\_limit\_reached}), also under parallel requests: the count is checked under a lock on the account row (projects) or the project row (versions). The list endpoints return the two numbers so the UI does not hard-code them.
\end{description}

\subsection*{Accounts}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.6cm,labelwidth=1.4cm,font=\bfseries\color{creBlue}]
\item[BR-15] No personal names are collected; a user is identified by e-mail. Organisation is optional.
\item[BR-16] E-mail OTP (sign-up and password reset): 6 digits, stored hashed, valid 10 minutes, at most 5 wrong attempts, resend at most once per 60 s. The account is created only after the OTP is verified. Google sign-up needs no OTP (Google verifies the e-mail); if its e-mail already belongs to an account, the Google id is linked to that account.
\item[BR-17] An account has one or two sign-in methods: password and/or Google. A user who registered with e-mail may link a Google account and unlink it later; a Google account is linked to at most one CRE account; a method can be removed only if another remains.
\item[BR-22] Password policy: at least 8 characters; every new password is entered twice and both entries must match. The strength bar is guidance only.
\item[BR-23] Without \emph{keep me logged in} the JWT cookie is a session cookie that ends when the browser closes; with it the cookie persists for a configured period (environment variable, default 30 days\assume). Log out (UC-03) always ends the session.
\end{description}

\subsection*{Audit log and admin access}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.6cm,labelwidth=1.4cm,font=\bfseries\color{creBlue}]
\item[BR-20] Every admin action is recorded in an append-only audit log: managing users (role change, deactivate, reactivate, delete), editing advice, editing RL thresholds, editing the $\RIC$/$\RIT$ tables, and requesting and downloading research exports. Each entry stores the time (UTC), the admin account, the action, the target (type and id), old and new values for changes, request id and IP address. The entry is written in the same transaction as the change, so no change happens without its entry. Entries cannot be edited or deleted through the application and are kept indefinitely\assume. If an admin account is later deleted (BR-13), its entries remain and show the account id.
\item[BR-21] Admins cannot open, view or change other users' projects, versions, answers, results or reports in the application. For each user they see only the number of projects and the number of assessment versions (not deleted\assume). Users' assessment data reaches admins only through the pseudonymised research export (UC-18).
\end{description}

\subsection*{Dashboard and presentation (v1.13)}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.6cm,labelwidth=1.4cm,font=\bfseries\color{creBlue}]
\item[BR-24] Expected progress $=\min(100,\max(0,\ \text{elapsed calendar days since start} / \text{duration} \times 100))$, rounded to a whole percent. On each submission the project's progress at that moment is stored with the version\assume{} and is used in version lists and comparisons.
\item[BR-25] Results views and the PDF show the disclaimer:\newline \thai{ผลลัพธ์เป็นข้อมูลเบื้องต้นสำหรับใช้ประกอบการพิจารณาเท่านั้น} (results are preliminary and for consideration only).
\item[BR-26] Dashboard figures use each non-deleted project's latest submitted version: H+VH count, level distribution, H/VH per project and Top 10 indicators most often H/VH (ties: more VH first, then code\assume). Total value sums the project values. Average $\RM$ of a version is the mean $\RM$ of its scored indicators; ``level changed'' compares indicators scored in both versions.
\end{description}

\subsection*{Configuration}
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.6cm,labelwidth=1.4cm,font=\bfseries\color{creBlue}]
\item[BR-18] The rounding settings $p_s$ and $p_m$ are set only by environment variables (e.g.\ \texttt{SCORE\_DECIMALS}, \texttt{RM\_DECIMALS}\assume) on api and worker; there is no admin UI. A changed value applies to versions created afterwards (BR-10).
\end{description}
